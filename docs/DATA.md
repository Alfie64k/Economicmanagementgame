# Data sources and limitations

* **Countries** (`data/countries.json`, from `tools/data_import/build_roster.py`): approximate 2023/24 values compiled by hand from public series (World Bank WDI, IMF WEO and Fiscal Monitor, OECD, UN WPP, national statistics offices). They are calibration inputs for a game, not authoritative statistics. Where a country runs structural surpluses (Norway, UAE, Singapore) debt is shown net-ish so interest costs are sensible. A live importer was not possible in the build environment (API hosts were not reachable); the table structure makes a scripted refresh straightforward.
* **Policies, projects, events, scenarios**: authored for the game (`tools/data_import/build_*.py`); effect sizes are balance parameters informed by orders of magnitude in the literature.
* **Map**: Natural Earth (public domain) 1:110m countries and 1:50m admin-1 units for nine federations, simplified by `tools/map_prep/build_map.py`. Singapore is added as a small polygon. Border depiction follows Natural Earth defaults and is not a political statement; a configurable border set is on the backlog.
* **Regions**: output, population and unemployment by region are generated deterministically from region ids and the national sector mix (see `Regions.cs`). They are illustrative only.

## Tax and benefit codes

* **Source** (`data/taxcodes.json`, from `tools/data_import/build_taxcodes.py`): approximate 2024/25 statutory income-tax allowances and bands, payroll and social-security contributions, corporation tax, VAT rates and category treatments, and headline benefit levels, compiled by hand from public statutory tables and payroll guides for each country (tax-authority publications and professional summaries), with a sample of rates re-checked against recent web sources (South Africa, India, Canada, Brazil, Norway, Singapore, Turkey, Korea) and OECD *Taxing Wages* as a plausibility reference. Figures are rounded to roughly two significant figures; they are gameplay calibration inputs, not tax advice. The generator holds the data as Python dicts, deep-validates it and fails (non-zero exit) if a country is missing, a band structure is malformed, benefit shares do not sum to one, or the implied average income-tax rate, payroll yield or VAT rate leaves a plausible range.
* **Structure**: one complete template per archetype (advanced, hub, emerging, resource, developing) in multiples of mean earnings ("me"), plus a per-country override that the loader deep-merges onto it (lists such as `bands` are replaced, not merged). Eight countries (GBR, USA, DEU, FRA, JPN, CHN, IND, BRA) and thirteen others whose thresholds are reliably known (KOR, AUS, CAN, ZAF, MEX, TUR, IDN, POL, CHL, RUS, NOR, SGP, CHE) carry full statutory detail in local currency ("lcu") with a stated mean annual earnings figure; the remaining seven (ARG, EGY, VNM, SAU, NGA, ARE, ETH) override only rates, ages, shares and VAT categories, with any bands or allowances expressed as multiples of an assumed mean wage and flagged as such in the label.
* **Limitations**: the code describes a single person on wage income. It ignores tax credits and deductions beyond the allowance (social-contribution deductibility, joint filing and family quotients, in-work credits, the 10% employment deduction in France, the minimum-wage exemption beyond its fold into the Turkish allowance), so average income-tax rates are overstated where those are large (Germany, France, Japan, Korea). Sub-national taxes are mostly ignored (US federal tax only; Canadian federal personal tax only; Switzerland uses a stylised federal-plus-average-cantonal schedule; Japan's resident tax is omitted). Where a schedule has more than seven brackets or a smooth tariff (Germany, Mexico, Korea, Singapore) adjacent brackets are merged so that tax is exact at the retained breakpoints. Employer contributions have a single tier, so capped employer systems (India, Singapore, Germany, France) are expressed as effective flat rates, and compulsory funded schemes (Australian superannuation, Singapore CPF, Chilean AFP) are counted as payroll even though the money is not government revenue. Reduced corporate rates that depend on turnover are converted to a profit-equivalent limit at a ~10% margin. Where a country has no reduced VAT rate, "reduced" is set to about half the standard rate (the United States uses 0.0 as specified). Benefit levels are typical annual amounts per recipient and the means-tested block is stylised (one withdrawal rate and work allowance per country); benefit shares are rough splits of social-benefit spending excluding health and are not reconciled to national accounts.
